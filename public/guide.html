What is Executive Brain?

Executive Brain is your external executive function assistant. It's designed specifically for people with ADHD who struggle with:

  • Task initiation - Starting tasks feels impossible
  • Prioritization - Everything feels equally urgent (or equally unimportant)
  • Time blindness - Tasks take longer than you think
  • Overwhelm - Too many things competing for attention
  • Productive procrastination - Doing other "productive" things to avoid the real task

The app connects to your TickTick task list and provides intelligent, ADHD-aware coaching to help you actually get things done.

Core Philosophy: Break everything down, reduce friction, and never shame. We problem-solve together.

How It Works

Executive Brain has three layers:

1. TickTick Integration

The app pulls your real tasks from TickTick, including priorities, due dates, and project organization. It knows what you should be doing based on your task list.

2. Focus Coaching

When you ask "What should I be doing?", the app doesn't just show you a list. It:

  • Identifies your #1 priority
  • Asks what you're actually doing
  • Helps you understand why you're avoiding (if you are)
  • Provides targeted strategies based on your specific blocker
  • Breaks tasks down until they're small enough to start

3. ADHD-Aware Strategies

Every response is designed for the ADHD brain:

  • Short, scannable messages
  • Clear next actions
  • No shame or judgment
  • Escape hatches when you're stuck
  • Celebration of small wins

Commands Reference

/now
The main command. Starts focus coaching by showing your #1 priority and asking what you're doing.
Also works: "What should I be doing?" or "What now?"
/add
Add a task with intelligent capture. Tell me what you need to do and I'll help break it down, pick the right project, and optionally help you get started.
Examples: "/add finish the report" or just "I need to call the dentist"
/checkin
Check in on your progress during a focus session. Reports how long you've been working and asks how it's going.
Use this when you want accountability or need to refocus.
/morning
Start your morning planning routine. Reviews today's tasks, checks overdue items, and helps set your top 3 priorities.
Best used first thing in the morning before diving into work.
/evening
Evening review routine. Celebrates wins, reviews what didn't get done (without judgment), and plans tomorrow.
Helps you close out the day and avoid carrying mental load overnight.
/intake
Brain dump mode. Get everything out of your head first, then organize it into tasks.
Great for when your head is full and you can't focus.
/stuck
When you're paralyzed and don't know where to start. Helps find the very next tiny action.
"What's the next physical action?" - just one step.

Focus Mode (The Core Feature)

Focus Mode is the heart of Executive Brain. Here's how the flow works:

Key insight: The goal isn't to shame you into working. It's to understand why starting feels hard and give you specific tools to overcome that specific barrier.

Avoidance Types & Strategies

When you're avoiding a task, there's always a reason. Executive Brain helps you identify which type of avoidance you're experiencing and provides targeted strategies:

1. Too Big
Strategies: 2-minute version, first step only, Swiss cheese method, break it down further
2. Unclear
Strategies: Clarify first, draft mode, rubber duck it, define what "done" looks like
3. Boring
Strategies: Body doubling, gamify it, pair with podcast/music, set a reward
4. Scary
Strategies: Permission to suck, lower the stakes, separate thinking from doing
5. Blocked
Strategies: Unblock it now, work around the blocker, escalate, mark as waiting
6. Distracted
Strategies: Brain dump first, phone away, close all tabs, body doubling
7. Low Energy
Strategies: Basic needs check, match task to energy, 5-min movement, reschedule
8. Overwhelmed
Strategies: Pick ONE thing, brain dump everything, ruthless triage, breathe first

Strategy Tracking (Learning What Works)

Executive Brain doesn't just suggest strategies - it learns which ones work for you. Every strategy has a score based on your personal outcomes.

How Scoring Works

  • Success (+3 points) - Strategy worked, you got unstuck and did the task
  • Partial (+1 point) - Strategy helped a bit but didn't fully work
  • Failure (-1 point) - Strategy didn't help this time

Personalized Suggestions

When you're avoiding a task, you'll see strategies sorted by what's worked best for you:

Example suggestion based on scores:
Based on what's worked for you before: 1. 2-Minute Version (score: +8) - Your go-to 2. First Step Only (score: +5) - Works well for "too big" 3. Permission to Suck (score: +2) - Still building data Which one do you want to try?

Recording Outcomes

Outcomes are recorded automatically when:

  • You try a strategy during avoidance coaching
  • A focus session ends
  • You mention what helped during evening review

Tweaking Strategies

When you discover something that makes a strategy work better, it gets saved as a "tweak":

  • "2-minute version works better with a physical timer"
  • "Body doubling is more effective with video calls than co-working spaces"
  • "Permission to suck helps most when I say it out loud"

The Goal: Over time, Executive Brain learns YOUR brain. What works for one person with ADHD might not work for another - this system adapts to you.

Daily Routines

Morning Planning

Start each day with intention:

  1. Review what's scheduled for today
  2. Check overdue tasks - what slipped and why?
  3. Identify your top 3 priorities (not 10, just 3)
  4. Time block your peak focus hours (typically 9am-12pm)
  5. Leave buffer time - you will underestimate

Evening Review

Close out the day properly:

  1. What got done? Celebrate wins!
  2. What didn't get done? (No judgment)
  3. Why didn't it get done? (Learn from it)
  4. Set tomorrow's top 3
  5. Brain dump anything still on your mind

Energy Pattern: Your peak focus window is typically mid-morning to noon when medication is most effective. Schedule demanding tasks here. Save routine/admin tasks for afternoon when energy dips.

ADHD Tips & Best Practices

Task Sizing

  • Tasks should be 15-30 minutes maximum
  • If a task feels big, break it down until it doesn't
  • The first step should be so small you can't say no

Time Estimation

  • You will underestimate. Always add 50% buffer.
  • "5 minutes" usually means 15 minutes
  • "30 minutes" usually means 45-60 minutes

When Stuck

  • Ask: "What's the next physical action?"
  • Try the 2-minute version
  • Body doubling helps - work alongside someone
  • Sometimes you need to step away (5-min walk)

Avoiding Shame Spirals

  • You're not lazy - you have ADHD
  • Missing a task isn't failure - it's data
  • Productive procrastination is still avoidance
  • Small wins count - celebrate them

Energy Zones

Your day is organized into energy zones that match your natural focus patterns:

  • Peak Focus (9am-12pm) - Deep work, demanding cognitive tasks
  • Recharge (12pm-2pm) - Lunch, breaks, reset time
  • Admin Time (2pm-5pm) - Emails, meetings, routine tasks
  • Wind Down (5pm-6pm) - Wrap up, planning for tomorrow

Technical Setup

Quick Start - macOS Apps (Easiest)

Double-clickable apps are installed in your Applications folder:

Executive Brain.app
Double-click to start all services and open the web UI automatically. Located at: /Applications/Executive Brain.app
Stop Executive Brain.app
Double-click to stop all services. Located at: /Applications/Stop Executive Brain.app

Tip: Drag these apps to your Dock for quick access!

Quick Start - Terminal

Start All Services
npm start
Stop All Services
npm stop
Check Status
npm run status
View Logs
npm run logs # All logs npm run logs:server # Server logs only npm run logs:processor # Processor logs only

Then open http://localhost:3001 in your browser.

Auto-Start on Login (Optional)

You can configure Executive Brain to start automatically when you log in:

Enable Auto-Start
./macos/install-autostart.sh
Disable Auto-Start
./macos/uninstall-autostart.sh

This installs a macOS Launch Agent that runs Executive Brain on login.

Rebuilding macOS Apps

If you need to rebuild the macOS apps (after code changes):

Build Apps
./macos/build-app.sh

How Commands Are Processed

  1. WebUI sends command to server via HTTP POST
  2. Server adds to .command-queue.json, returns commandId immediately
  3. Processor detects queue change, processes command
  4. Processor fetches data from TickTick via MCP
  5. Processor writes response to .command-responses.json
  6. Server detects response, broadcasts via WebSocket
  7. WebUI receives WebSocket message (or polling fallback), displays response

Memory System

Executive Brain remembers context between sessions in memories.json:

  • preferences - Your peak focus time, task size preference
  • taskPatterns - Common blockers, energy patterns, procrastination triggers
  • strategies - ADHD coping strategies with scores and outcomes
  • eveningReviews - Historical data from evening reviews

  • public/app.js - Frontend logic with WebSocket + polling
  • public/styles.css - Shared styling

Backend (src/):

  • src/server.js - Express + WebSocket server
  • src/processor.js - Watches queue, processes commands
  • ecosystem.config.js - PM2 process configuration

macOS Integration (macos/):

  • macos/brain-launcher.sh - Shell wrapper (loads nvm, starts services)
  • macos/ExecutiveBrain.applescript - Start app source
  • macos/StopExecutiveBrain.applescript - Stop app source
  • macos/build-app.sh - Builds .app bundles
  • macos/install-autostart.sh - Enable launch agent
  • macos/uninstall-autostart.sh - Disable launch agent

Data Files:

  • .command-queue.json - Pending commands from WebUI
  • .command-responses.json - Responses for WebUI
  • data/memories.json - User patterns, strategies, and context
  • data/projects/ - Project markdown files
  • logs/ - PM2 log files
